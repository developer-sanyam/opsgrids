# ADR-0035 — List prices 2026-10-06: INR 3,00,000 a year, INR 25,000 a month, for every product

**Status:** Accepted (owner decision, 2026-10-06; the monthly reading is to be confirmed before merge — see
Context). Built on `feat/list-prices-30k-month-3l-year` (the branch name predates the monthly reading below), not
merged or deployed · **Date:** 2026-10-06 · **Services:** `catalog` (migration, publish check), `subscription`
(customer subscribe, cadence refusal copy), `console` (MRR by cadence), `frontend/opsgrids-dashboard` (Marketplace
trial pin), `frontend/opsgrids-internal` (cadence guidance) · **Design:**
[list prices](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-06-list-prices-design.md)

Builds on [ADR-0000](0000-phase-0-decisions.md) §8 (money is integer minor units) and §9 (plan versions are
immutable; a subscription pins a version); [ADR-0004](0004-commercial-model.md) §2 (re-pricing is a new
version; a bespoke price is an audience-of-one version); [ADR-0013](0013-self-serve-products-and-the-one-box-deployment.md)
§2 (each product sells as a flat price per organisation, its amount is deployment data the owner sets);
[ADR-0029](0029-staff-plan-control.md) §8 and [ADR-0034](0034-customer-plan-change-and-plan-visibility.md) §3
(what is offered is the newest published version per audience, cadence and currency).

## Context

Each of the eleven sellable products has one public flat base plan, seeded with a **placeholder** monthly price
(`e1f2a3b4c5d6`, `10e6b62d9ac7`, `53f1a7a8b99d`) that production still sold on 2026-10-06. Together they came to
INR 19,989 a month. The owner's decision, in his words: "For total billing of complete product should be
300000rs years and monthly so divided according to that only into the all the products" — every product
together costs **INR 3,00,000 a year**, the monthly price is **divided from that: INR 25,000 a month**
(3,00,000 / 12), and each total is divided across the products in proportion to the current prices. There was
no yearly price at all.

**Reading, and what still needs the owner's word.** The sentence fixes one number, the yearly total, and derives
the monthly one from it; this record takes it literally (monthly = yearly / 12, no yearly discount). An earlier
draft of this branch read it as INR 30,000 a month with a year priced as ten months — a number the owner never
wrote, which would have billed every monthly customer 20% more than the literal reading. **Confirm before
merging** that INR 25,000 a month is meant; if a discounted year was meant instead, only the monthly column of
`NEW_PRICES` changes (the yearly column is the owner's number either way). Also confirm that the per-seat
Starter/Growth plans and the suite bundles staying at their old prices is intended ("Not changed").

Verified in code before deciding how to ship it:

1. A published version is frozen below the application (triggers `549e7957161c`, `e3f8a91c7b2d`,
   `b7e4d2a9c613`); since `b7e4d2a9c613` nothing can even be *inserted* under one. A price is changed by
   publishing a new version, never by editing the old one, and the old one stays published (nothing
   grandfathers on publish).
2. The offerable set (`selectors.offerable_versions`, the staff and customer plan pickers and both change-plan
   writes) keeps the newest published version per (audience, cadence, currency), so a new monthly version
   supersedes the old one and a yearly version is offered beside it as its own cadence.
3. Three reads take "the newest published public version with a flat price" as a product's list price **without
   looking at cadence**: `get_public_flat_plan` (bespoke-price clone source), `all_public_flat_plans` (console's
   pricing baseline and product names), `current_public_versions` (the Plans page's "sells today").
4. The dashboard Marketplace's `trialOffer` pinned a new trial to the **first** (oldest) published flat version —
   after any re-price, every new trial would have started on the old price.
5. The customer `subscribe` write accepted any published version of the product by id, superseded ones included;
   the change-plan writes enforce the offerable set (ADR-0034 §3), subscribe did not. The customer plan read lists
   every published version's id and `version_no`.
6. A subscription keeps one cadence for life: both change-plan writes refuse a monthly-to-yearly move with code
   `cadence` (`domain.assert_same_cadence`), and the customer picker blocks it with the same code. Billing prices
   a period as `amount_minor x quantity` whatever its length, and the billing run rolls a yearly subscription
   every 365 days. The refusal's sentence (and the console's guidance for it) told the reader to cancel and
   subscribe again — which loses the product, because a cancelled subscription cannot be replaced.
7. Console's MRR fold (`price.resolve_plan_price`) summed a version's flat prices **whatever its cadence**, so a
   yearly subscriber's annual fee counted as a month's revenue in the org snapshot, platform MRR, the series and
   the "Per month" column. Before this change a yearly version could only be a bespoke price.
8. A draft's `version_no` is taken from the same sequence as everything else on the plan, and publishing a draft
   did not check that a newer version had already been published for its audience, cadence and currency — a
   draft published below one is "published" (audit row, event) and never offered or shown as the price.

## Decision

1. **The prices** (integer paise; yearly sums to exactly 30,000,000 = INR 3,00,000; each monthly price is exactly
   its yearly price / 12, and they sum to exactly 2,500,000 = INR 25,000):

   | Product (`product_key` / plan `code`) | Was, paise / month | Monthly, paise | Yearly, paise |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | Revenue (`crm` / `revenue`) | 1,99,900 | 2,50,000 | 30,00,000 |
   | People (`hrms` / `people`) | 1,99,900 | 2,50,000 | 30,00,000 |
   | IT (`itsm` / `it`) | 1,99,900 | 2,50,000 | 30,00,000 |
   | Finance (`finance` / `finance`) | 2,49,900 | 3,12,500 | 37,50,000 |
   | Inventory (`operations` / `inventory`) | 2,49,900 | 3,12,500 | 37,50,000 |
   | Engage (`engage` / `engage`) | 1,49,900 | 1,87,500 | 22,50,000 |
   | Marketing (`marketing` / `marketing`) | 1,49,900 | 1,87,500 | 22,50,000 |
   | Projects (`projects` / `projects`) | 1,49,900 | 1,87,500 | 22,50,000 |
   | PulseBoard (`pulseboard` / `pulseboard`) | 1,49,900 | 1,87,500 | 22,50,000 |
   | Storefront (`storefront` / `storefront`) | 1,49,900 | 1,87,500 | 22,50,000 |
   | Support (`support` / `support`) | 1,49,900 | 1,87,500 | 22,50,000 |
   | **All eleven** | **19,98,900** | **25,00,000** | **3,00,00,000** |

   Each is within 0.02% of its exact proportional share, rounded so the totals are exact.
2. **Shipped as a catalog data migration, `b88a833020dc`**, in the shape of the flat-plan seeds — not typed into
   the Plans page — so every environment agrees and the numbers are reviewed in a diff. For each of the eleven
   plans it inserts two **published, public** versions: the **yearly** one at the plan's highest `version_no` + 1,
   then the **monthly** one at + 2. Each has one `flat` INR price and clones the features (values exactly as
   stored) and free seat classes of the plan's newest published public version. Deterministic `uuid5` ids;
   re-runnable.
3. **Inserts only.** Each version is inserted as a draft, its sheet written, then published (`draft ->
   published`, the step every operator publish takes); no published row is touched. The versions in force before
   stay published and pinned: **existing subscriptions keep their price** until staff switch them.
4. **The monthly version is numbered last** so the three cadence-blind reads (Context 3) stay on the monthly
   list price, which is what they meant before a yearly price existed.
5. **The Marketplace starts a trial on the newest public monthly version** (Context 4), falling back to the plan's
   newest published flat version (never its oldest, which the server would now refuse) only for a plan with no
   public monthly version. Logic only; no UI change.
6. **Refused, not guessed.** Every plan is checked before anything is inserted. A source sheet that is not exactly
   one flat INR price, or has a paid seat class, aborts the whole migration; so does an **open public draft** on a
   plan it re-prices (Context 8), and the error names every one — delete each on the Plans page (or publish it
   first, knowing this release supersedes it at once) and re-run. A bespoke draft is its own audience and does not
   block. A retired plan, a plan with no published public version (withdrawn on purpose) and a missing plan are
   skipped with a warning; a source selling at another amount than the split was computed from (`SEEDED_MINOR`)
   is applied with a warning. The warnings go to the root logger, because `env.py`'s `fileConfig` disables every
   other one that already exists — alembic's own included — and they would otherwise never reach the migrate
   output.
7. **Downgrade refuses** while any of these versions exists, unless `OPSGRIDS_ALLOW_LIST_PRICE_DOWNGRADE=1`:
   catalog cannot see subscription's pins (no cross-schema read), and deleting a pinned version breaks billing
   and every plan read for its subscribers. With the override it deletes exactly what it inserted. Its own
   variable, not the shared `OPSGRIDS_ALLOW_DESTRUCTIVE_DOWNGRADE`: `tools/roundtrip.sh` must pass it to every
   service's `downgrade base` (the refusal fires on any seeded database), and the shared one there would also
   switch off operations' refusals to drop real lots, rolls and put-away captures.
8. **No outbox event and no audit row**, like every seeded plan; this record and the migration are the trail.
   The one consumer that misses the event is **notification**: `catalog.plan_version.published` is subscribable
   for automation rules (`notification/app/domain.py`), so a rule on it does not fire for these 22 versions —
   whoever deploys announces the change by hand.
9. **The customer subscribe write enforces the offerable set** (Context 5), as change-plan does: a version a newer
   one of its plan supersedes is refused `422 not_offerable` and nothing is created; an unreachable catalog is a
   503. A dashboard tab loaded before the deploy, or anyone reading `version_no`, can no longer start on the old
   price. A version negotiated for the organization is its own audience and is still taken. The staff Add product
   path is unchanged (its picker already offers only the offerable set).
10. **Console counts a month's worth in MRR** (Context 7): the resolved price is the flat total for one period
    divided by the months it covers — a yearly price / 12, rounded half up in integer paise — and 0 for
    `one_time`, which the billing run never bills. The org snapshot, platform MRR, the revenue log the series
    replays and the "Per month" column all take that number.
11. **Publishing refuses a version that would be superseded on arrival** (Context 8): `422` with code
    `superseded` on field `version_no`, naming the newer version to start a draft from. "Superseded" is exactly
    `offerable_versions`' rule (same plan, audience, cadence and currency, higher number, published), so a yearly
    version beside a monthly draft, a public one above a bespoke draft, or a withdrawn one never blocks.
12. **The cadence refusal no longer advises cancelling** (Context 6): the server's sentence and the console's
    guidance say a subscription keeps its cadence, moving between cadences is not possible yet, and cancelling
    does not open a way.

## Consequences

* A new customer's trial, and staff Add product, start on the new monthly price; staff Add product can also
  start a product on the yearly price. The customer's own Change plan offers the new monthly price (an upgrade,
  prorated now on a paying subscription; immediate and free on a trial) and shows the yearly one blocked with
  `cadence`. List prices are still never sent to a tenant (ADR-0034 §5).
* Staff move an existing customer to the new monthly price with Change plan (ADR-0029: a trial now, a paying
  subscription at its next period). **A monthly subscription cannot become yearly**: every write refuses
  `cadence`, and the only path — cancel, then subscribe — is not built (a cancelled row cannot be replaced yet).
* The old versions stay published for the subscribers pinned to them, but nothing new can start on them: no
  screen posts them, and the customer subscribe write refuses them (Decision 9). Withdrawing them on the Plans
  page (ADR-0030) is no longer needed to close that path.
* A yearly subscriber shows in MRR at a twelfth of the yearly price. One folded before this console build keeps
  the full amount until its next transition (the backfill does not re-fold a snapshot it already holds); the
  RUNBOOK's pre-deploy check counts them — before this change only a bespoke price could be yearly.
* The cadence-blind reads stay correct only while the monthly version is the newest. If a yearly price is
  re-published alone later, they would show it as the product's price.

## Not changed

The per-seat Starter/Growth plans, the suite bundles, every bespoke version, every subscription, the billing run
and the staff subscribe path. Confirm with the owner that leaving the per-seat plans and the bundles at their old
prices is meant ("complete product" could include them).

## Follow-ups (recorded, not built)

1. **Monthly to yearly for an existing customer** needs cancel-and-replace (or a cadence change with its own
   proration rule), and today a cancelled subscription cannot be replaced.
2. **The dashboard cannot start a product on yearly**: the Marketplace has no cadence choice (it shows no price at
   all — pricing is agreed per client). Staff Add product can.
3. **Make the three "public flat plan" reads cadence-aware** (prefer `month`) instead of relying on version order.
